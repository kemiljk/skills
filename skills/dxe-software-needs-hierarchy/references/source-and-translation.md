# Source and translation notes

Source checked 9 November 2026.

## Primary source

A. H. Maslow, [*A Theory of Human Motivation*](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Maslow/motivation) (1943), *Psychological Review*, 50, 370–396. York University's Classics in the History of Psychology hosts the article. [DOI record](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0054346).

The paper describes physiological, safety, love, esteem, and self-actualisation needs. Its sections on the degree of fixity and relative satisfaction qualify their ordering: exceptions occur, satisfaction is partial, and emergence is gradual. It also recognises that a behaviour can serve several needs. Use those qualifications rather than presenting an inflexible staircase.

## Our software interpretation

The five software layers, their questions, and the prioritisation method are an editorial synthesis. Maslow did not propose this software framework. We use the analogy to expose dependencies between useful outcomes, dependable operation, contextual fit, agency, and meaningful possibilities.

- **Basic function and access:** people need to accomplish the promised task before dependent enhancements can help them.
- **Safety and trust:** people need dependable behaviour, protection of work, and control over consequences.
- **Fit and belonging:** people should be accommodated by the product's language, conventions, and workflows. Social functionality is conditional on purpose.
- **Competence and confidence:** people should understand consequences and develop practical capability, rather than merely receive praise.
- **Growth and expression:** people should pursue their own goals, including creative freedom or simply reclaiming time.

This is a decision aid, not an empirically established universal ranking, a psychological diagnosis, or a compliance standard. Translate needs for a named person and task; do not infer emotions from interface appearance. Accessibility, craft, and performance can affect multiple layers. Serious safety risks can take precedence over ordinary functional improvements, and expressive capability can be foundational in a creative product.
