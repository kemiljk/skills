# Portfolio advice from public videos and another podcast

Four original public recordings were downloaded normally and transcribed locally with Apple SpeechTranscriber on 17 October 2026. Selected transcript text was reviewed; no human listened to verify the audio, and no speaker diarization or rendered-video inspection was performed. Notes describe the spoken account, not a visual review of the slides or portfolios shown. [Source and transcription provenance](video-portfolio-sources.json) records dates, hashes, and reviewed ranges. Full transcripts and media stay outside the distributed skill.

These examples calibrate judgment rather than supply a universal format. The sources include personal experience and hiring preferences; none establishes that a portfolio feature caused an employment outcome.

## VA01 — Demonstrate readiness for a first management role

[Femke: The portfolio presentation that got me hired as a design manager](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TxBrcdiNqcM) · 7 December 2022 · 06:23–09:18, 11:33–12:29, 12:29–14:37.

Femke describes moving from an individual contributor role to management. She connects existing team initiatives, coaching, and facilitation to management responsibilities, and explains her relevant experience and motivation. For the project discussion, she reports opening actual Figma files to show how she worked. This is her account of a successful interview presentation, not a controlled hiring study.

**Apply:** for a first management transition, recognise concrete evidence of enabling other people and leading relevant work even without a prior manager title. Describe what that evidence supports and which management responsibilities remain unproven. Tailor a permitted walkthrough to the job and interview brief.

**Boundaries:** do not require public speaking, community mentoring, paid mentoring sessions, side projects, a fixed deck structure, or access to confidential working files. Coaching examples do not prove every responsibility of managing direct reports. Interview material need not all appear on the public website.

## VA02 — A concise introduction can retain useful depth

[Femke: Showcases: the new portfolio case study](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zMGah34LgnU) · 1 March 2025 · 00:18–02:24, 03:56–06:01, 06:05–09:57.

Femke distinguishes initial screening from a later project discussion. She recommends making the problem, personal role, output, and impact easy to skim, with optional links to depth. She explicitly allows qualitative outcomes when exact metrics are unavailable and describes presenting a body of work without forcing each item into a lengthy case study.

**Apply:** preserve a concise project or tenure summary when it supplies relevant, attributable evidence. Add depth where a consequential decision or claim needs explanation; an optional linked case study can serve that purpose.

**Boundaries:** her peer poll, twenty-second framing, and suggested paragraph count are contextual advice, not measured universal thresholds. Example percentage improvements are illustrative, not outcomes to copy. Do not require motion, cut all reasoning, or force every project into the same format. Qualitative benefit still needs honest supporting evidence; shipment alone does not prove it.

## VA03 — Make student work understandable without a compulsory playback

[NN/g: 7 Portfolio Mistakes That Could Hurt Your UX Job Search](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/7-portfolio-mistakes/) · Megan Brown · 24 March 2025 · 01:37–02:40, 02:40–05:05, 05:05–06:27.

Brown discusses student UX portfolios. Her examples distinguish a personal redesign from employment, clarify individual contributions to group work, and explain why a video, prototype, or exported class slides can lose context when read independently. A tailored portfolio deck can itself be a useful format.

**Apply:** give a reader enough accessible context to understand the project, candidate’s role, key reasoning, and result without requiring them to play a video or explore a prototype. Adapt presentation material for its audience and medium. Label school, concept, and self-initiated projects accurately.

**Boundaries:** the source’s recommendation for three or four projects and its internship-specific expectations are not a minimum project count for every portfolio. A prototype remains meaningful evidence when relevant. Inspect the actual material before reporting inaccessible text, missing credits, or other defects; the format alone does not establish a failure.

## VA04 — Judge brand work by its communication job

[Design Life 315: How to hire (and get hired) in a brand design role](https://www.designlife.fm/episode/315-how-to-hire-and-get-hired-in-a-brand-design-role) · Femke and Charli · 28 July 2025 · 08:08–13:45, 16:14–18:59, 19:00–23:23, 23:44–25:50, 26:41–28:49.

The hosts discuss actual hiring needs in their respective teams. They examine the message an asset must convey, its audience and context, consistency across a brand, iteration on existing work, and individual contribution within a campaign. They distinguish exceptional craft from the particular medium and responsibilities a role needs. Their performance discussion explicitly accepts attempts to obtain results when clients or organisations do not provide them.

**Apply:** for brand and motion roles, connect visual choices to a communication purpose and relevant medium. Assess contribution, collaboration, feedback, and what was learned about performance. An honest account of trying to obtain unavailable results can demonstrate care without inventing numbers.

**Boundaries:** these selected hiring preferences do not exclude agency backgrounds, mandate new unpaid personal projects, or establish that missing public examples mean no ability. Do not impose product-interface artifacts on brand work, infer measurable impact from stated intent, or judge a candidate as indifferent simply because results were unavailable.

## Resolve apparent disagreements through the task

VA02 and VA03 address different contexts and amounts of evidence. Use their shared concern—can this reader understand relevant work?—rather than declaring showcases or long case studies universally correct. Keep an optional walkthrough optional when the existing material already communicates the necessary evidence. Review leadership, brand, product, and implementation work against the responsibilities the candidate is trying to demonstrate.
