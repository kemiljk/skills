## DM01 — Work, taste, and current practice

**Source:** Karl’s six supplied screenshots of Danny’s article headed “Stop asking designers…”. Original URL and date are unverified; author and handle are visible. Relevant material appears in photos 1 and 4–6.

Danny questions résumé and interview rituals that reveal little about the work. He suggests examining output, asking which products a designer admires and why, and understanding how they work now. His example about tax software values a specific explanation of making a difficult task accessible. The name-only résumé is a reported anecdote, not a recommended application format.

**Apply:** inspect actual work; recognise taste through reasons, choices, and consequences for users. Connect a workflow to the candidate’s own judgment and feedback, including when AI is involved.

**Boundaries:** do not require favourite fashionable brands, a taste page, a workflow essay, or a name-only résumé. Preserve a CV required by an application. Claims that human experience cannot be prompted are opinion, not an AI-detection method. Historical and application-volume claims are not part of the bar.

## DM02 — Mutual evaluation through the work

[5 tips for hiring designers in 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/5-tips-hiring-designers-2026-danny-martinez-9wgbc) · 1 October 2026 · primary author article, also supplied as six screenshots. These are the same source.

Danny prioritises work over pedigree, describes Claire Taylor choosing between paid evaluation options at Superpower, and reports Robert Floyd responding to Arrows’ candid introduction before a paid project and eventual hire. He advocates early conversations about work, honest expectations, flexibility, and respect for candidates’ choices.

**Apply:** when hiring-process or interview preparation is explicitly requested, connect each step to the evidence it supplies. A bounded, fairly paid collaboration can reveal how people exchange feedback and work together; candidates also evaluate the team.

**Boundaries:** the article’s salary figures, three-day trial, and interview sequence describe a selected network and examples, not universal requirements. Preserve workable alternatives and candidate availability. Do not add unpaid production assignments, promise offers, or turn a portfolio review into a process audit. Existing projects and a permitted walkthrough may supply the needed evidence.

## DM03 — Employer prestige is context in either direction

[Don’t hire based on brand name alone](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/danny-felipe-martinez_the-best-hires-may-not-have-worked-at-google-activity-7326254472005918720-9zhL) · public author post introducing an interview with Jessica Toy; exact publication date unverified.

Danny questions using famous employers as a shortcut. He explicitly says a prestigious background neither rules someone out nor supplies enough reason to hire them.

**Apply:** inspect the person’s actual work, decisions, and fit for the target problem. This also qualifies the anti-big-company reactions reported in DM06: those reactions are hiring-market observations, not a rule to reproduce.

**Boundary:** long tenure cannot establish stale thinking. Current ability may be visible in permitted employer work; side projects and unpaid work are not compulsory proof.

## DM04 — Examine judgment in an AI-assisted workflow

[Hiring Humans Tactics](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/danny-felipe-martinez_hiring-humans-tactics-activity-7247466652512980992-P-KU) · public author post; exact publication date unverified. The linked newsletter was inaccessible and adds no evidence here.

Danny suggests allowing AI tools while examining how a person thinks and prompts, using work portfolios rather than documents alone, and giving interviewees focused human attention.

**Apply:** where relevant, ask what the candidate framed, selected, corrected, tested, and learned through feedback. Describe the actual demonstrated capability.

**Boundary:** a tool list or fluent prompt is not proof of product judgment, production engineering, or a successful outcome. AI use alone is not evidence of weak effort. Do not add a compulsory AI-tool requirement or an undisclosed automated candidate assessment.

## DM05 — Read the direction of the claim

[Entry-level hiring post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/danny-felipe-martinez_we-dont-need-to-hire-entry-level-people-activity-7295766214918717441-MV-l) · public author commentary and summary of a Peter Wood interview; exact publication date unverified.

Danny criticises the idea that AI removes the need for junior hiring and highlights the longer-term talent pipeline. The opening anti-junior statement is a view he challenges.

**Apply:** evaluate early-career evidence at the relevant level and recognise learning and initiative without importing senior ownership requirements.

**Boundary:** this is not a measured labour-market forecast or an assurance of available jobs. Do not attribute the quoted claim to Danny as his recommendation.

## DM06 — Replace vague titles with actual responsibilities

[Dive Club 184](https://rss.com/podcasts/diveclub/3184290) · 29 September 2026 · 01:20–03:47, 09:24–11:28, 12:05–16:15, 30:00–33:16, 36:47–44:43. Selected local machine-transcript passages; not verified by human listening.

Danny asks what a company wants without using the title “design engineer”. The discussion distinguishes design-led prototyping from engineering depth and considers reusable brand tools that help other people produce work. It also reports biases toward large-company backgrounds and familiar portfolios.

**Apply:** match demonstrated responsibilities to the actual job: product decisions, prototype iteration, production code, or a usable brand system are distinct signals. Make the candidate’s contribution to a system and its users traceable.

**Boundaries:** the same title does not imply the same duties. Neither tool use nor a polished demo proves production depth. Reported employer and AI-authorship suspicions are not evidence about a particular candidate. Read DM03 alongside those anecdotes. This is the same episode as DC184, with overlapping candidates and examples from DM02.

## DM07 — Human context and candidate agency

[Design Discipline — Danny Martinez](https://www.designdisciplin.com/p/danny-martinez) · 28 September 2026 · 10:04–12:28, 15:37–17:35, 30:53–33:12, 42:25–47:48, 53:27–57:10, 58:14–58:46. Original public audio transcribed locally with Apple SpeechTranscriber; selected text reviewed, without human audio verification.

Danny describes candidates choosing whether to accept introductions, clarifying the opportunities they want, and using work to support trust. He values a portfolio conveying something specific about a person’s thinking and sensibility, and again emphasises working together over prolonged interview performance.

**Apply:** retain a candidate’s useful voice, meaningful choices, and working context. For interview guidance, treat fit as a decision for both sides.

**Boundaries:** warmth, vulnerability, humour, and a particular aesthetic are preferences, not universal competence tests. Do not demand personal disclosure or infer AI authorship from style. The host’s comments about sales-oriented portfolio layouts are the host’s perspective, not Danny’s rule. Client conversion can be appropriate for a freelance portfolio. Success stories remain selected commercial-network accounts.
