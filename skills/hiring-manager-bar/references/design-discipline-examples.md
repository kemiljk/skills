# Design Discipline: evidence appropriate to the work

These examples calibrate judgments; they do not add a compulsory portfolio format or set of tools. [Coverage and provenance](design-discipline-coverage.json) distinguish public articles, publisher highlights, and locally generated machine transcripts. Selected transcript passages were read as text, without human audio verification or speaker diarization. Danny Martinez’s episode is already curated as DM07 and is not counted again here.

## DD01 — Relevant selection and a contextual application letter

[Irmak Nur Sunal Lutkin](https://www.designdisciplin.com/p/irmak) · 5 September 2022 · 24:49–28:09, 33:08–36:36, 37:35–38:57.

Irmak values relevant, selected work over padding a portfolio to reach an arbitrary count. In her hiring context, she also describes a concise, useful cover letter as important, especially when interpreting less polished junior evidence. She describes collaboration and research responsibilities varying with the project and team.

**Apply:** connect the selection to the actual role and level. Make personal contribution visible without requiring every designer to conduct every activity alone. Preserve an explicitly requested application artifact.

**Boundaries:** her preference for cover letters is contextual; it does not make one compulsory for every application. Equally, Danny’s preference for work-first screening does not override an employer’s stated application requirements. Example project counts are not quotas. Her employer role is historical context at publication, not a verified current position. Do not equate polished senior work with the appropriate junior threshold.

## DD02 — Judge the experience of opening the work

[Dan Hollick](https://www.designdisciplin.com/p/daniel-hollick) · 25 June 2026 · 38:59–40:35, 42:10–43:39, 47:02–52:23.

Dan says his writing helped lead to Tailwind documentation work, and clarifies that joining Tailwind preceded Making Software. The interview also discusses portfolio access friction. The host voices an absolute objection to Figma portfolios; Dan, who previously used one, discusses extra steps and delays for a busy reviewer.

**Apply:** recognise relevant evidence in writing, diagrams, or another artifact when it demonstrates the target duties. Inspect the actual route to opening and understanding work: access, loading, navigation, and image clarity.

**Boundaries:** a tool name or deck format alone cannot establish a bad experience. Verify actual restrictions and behaviour before reporting a defect; source extraction cannot measure performance. The host’s rejection preference is not a universal rule. Reported hires and the effect of online visibility remain selected accounts; an audience and extensive unpaid side projects are not mandatory. Do not claim Making Software caused the earlier Tailwind hire.

## DD03 — Career visibility is advice, rather than a portfolio requirement

[Ridd](https://www.designdisciplin.com/p/ridd) · 26 May 2026 · 30:06–32:29, 59:58–64:32, 74:27–75:21.

Ridd describes building relationships and sharing work as helpful in his career. He values care in the products he builds, while the discussion also includes broad claims about internal tools, large-company experience, and changes in hiring.

**Apply:** useful relationships and visible work can be optional routes to opportunity when career advice is requested. Judge the portfolio’s actual evidence of care, decisions, and relevant capability.

**Boundaries:** audience size, frequent posting, extroversion, and social proximity are not competence criteria. Internal-tool work does not imply poor craft, and long tenure at a large employer does not establish stale ability. His commercial-network observations are selected experience, rather than a measured market-wide rule. Do not mistake the conversation’s forecasts or personality preferences for validated hiring signals. No new portfolio requirement is derived from these passages.

## DD04 — Make the reason for a design understandable

[Adrian Shaughnessy](https://www.designdisciplin.com/p/adrian-shaughnessy) · 29 October 2025 · 44:43–47:18, 52:58–54:59, 59:16–63:47.

Adrian values explaining the intent and value of design choices, informed by his experience writing readable proposals and discussing work with clients. He encourages students to retain work they care about and describes critique as part of developing and defending ideas.

**Apply:** preserve distinctive work while making its purpose and the candidate’s reasons understandable. Use a concise explanation, annotated artifact, or permitted walkthrough suited to the audience and stage. When relevant, show how critique changed a decision.

**Boundaries:** a client presentation’s sequence is not a requirement to bury artifacts behind a long public case study. Academic language, native English, a confident performance, and a particular presentation medium are not substitutes for relevant evidence. This is professional advice and personal experience, not a measured hiring intervention. His meeting/outreach suggestions do not authorise contacting anyone or require every candidate to follow that approach.

## DD05 — A broad title contains different jobs

[Design Engineering 101](https://www.designdisciplin.com/p/design-engineering-101) · Baytaş · 17 September 2024 · public authored article, “What Do Design Engineers Do?”

Baytaş describes work across marketing sites, product refinement and systems, exploratory prototypes, and early-stage product building. He also describes tools that help other designers and collaboration across complementary disciplines.

**Apply:** resolve the job’s actual duties before deciding which artifacts matter. A research prototype can demonstrate exploration; a reusable component system can demonstrate interface implementation; a marketing site may demonstrate communication and interaction craft. Identify what each artifact actually establishes.

**Boundaries:** these categories are a useful lens, rather than a requirement to demonstrate all four or work alone. A prototype does not establish production reliability. Do not import historical job-market forecasts, tool recommendations, or accessibility compromises as current hiring criteria.

## DD06 — A prototype can explain unfamiliar work

[Håkan Lidbo](https://www.designdisciplin.com/p/hakan-lidbo) · 31 March 2021 · publisher-written introduction and “On Categories” highlights, rather than a reviewed full transcript.

The public highlights describe Lidbo’s work spanning music, installations, games, robots, and software. The publisher argues that showing the work and using prototypes can communicate ideas that are hard to summarise within a familiar category.

**Apply:** allow a clear artifact or demonstration to establish what unusual work is and why it matters. Add the minimum explanation needed to identify the person’s contribution, collaborators, purpose, and status.

**Boundaries:** novelty and category resistance are not competence tests. Do not replace useful context with the claim that work always speaks for itself, or force interdisciplinary work into a conventional product case-study template. The publisher’s interpretation is distinct from Lidbo’s quoted remarks. No full-episode conclusions are inferred from highlights.
