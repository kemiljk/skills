# Dive Club: portfolio and hiring examples

Use these examples to recognise useful evidence and make bounded recommendations. They supplement Karl's bar. They do not establish hiring probabilities, prescribe a visual style, or replace inspecting the candidate's actual work.

These are original paraphrases of selected passages in locally generated Apple SpeechTranscriber transcripts, researched on 17 October 2026. They have been reviewed as text, not verified by human listening. Names and episode identities use publisher metadata where available. Timestamps refer to the downloaded audio; advertisements can shift them. See the [coverage record](dive-club-coverage.json) and [research method](dive-club-research.md).

## DC143 — Matt Sellers: deliberately select the strongest work

[Episode 143](https://rss.com/podcasts/diveclub/2474921) · 21 January 2026 · 01:25–03:45, 04:57–07:30. **Evidence:** candidate account; linked to the hire discussed in DC136.

Matt describes removing most of his older work, then reducing the selection again. Brief case-study introductions lead into carefully selected artifacts. His intended signal was current craft, rather than the accumulated size of his career.

**Review action:** identify which pieces strengthen the candidate's stated direction; consider removing distracting or weaker material before requesting more projects or process text.

**Boundary:** his craft-focused strategy does not justify removing useful context or impact evidence from every portfolio. This and DC136 describe the same hire.

## DC136 — Nad Chishtie: what resonated with the hiring manager

[Episode 136](https://rss.com/podcasts/diveclub/2400906) · 19 December 2025 · 18:30–24:00, 34:17–39:10. **Evidence:** Lovable hiring manager's account of Matt's portfolio and stated screening preferences.

Nad praises specific introductory writing, clear visual execution, and intentional interaction details. He accepts side projects as craft evidence, while distinguishing the teamwork questions they leave open. He also asks candidates to clarify their own contribution instead of attributing everything to an undefined team.

**Review action:** connect claims to visible work and personal ownership; explain what an experiment actually demonstrates.

**Boundary:** early screening can defer deeper process questions to conversation. It does not prove collaboration, shipped impact, or fit for every employer.

## DC150 — Tommy Smith: let relevant self-initiated work lead

[Episode 150](https://rss.com/podcasts/diveclub/2552458) · 16 February 2026 · 06:34–11:40, 30:00–33:30. **Evidence:** candidate account and Ridd's account of admitting him to the talent network; the introduction reports a subsequent job.

Tommy gives his calendar project, Chrono, prominent placement because it better represents the work he wants to do. Ridd says trying it revealed ability that the employer history had not communicated. Embedded demonstrations also let visitors understand interactions without opening the project.

**Review action:** surface a relevant experiment and make its behavior inspectable; label its status and ownership.

**Boundary:** network admission and a reported job do not establish that this placement caused the offer or that a prototype proves production delivery.

## DC182 — Nev Flynn: depth can fit a real team need

[Episode 182](https://rss.com/podcasts/diveclub/3155343) · 15 September 2026 · 01:02–03:20, 41:12–43:30. **Evidence:** ElevenLabs hiring manager's account and preferences.

Nev describes adding specialists, including sound and WebGL expertise, to enable work the team otherwise could not make. For portfolios, he values distinctive recent work over a long archive.

**Review action:** recognise a demonstrated specialism and relate it to the target team's needs; do not default to recommending broader skills or additional projects.

**Boundary:** this team context differs from Lovable's emphasis on breadth. Neither preference establishes the right shape for every designer. The anonymous interaction example in the later discussion may overlap with DC184; do not count it as independent corroboration.

## DC162 — Polly D’Arcy: preserve depth for the interview

[Episode 162](https://rss.com/podcasts/diveclub/2774321) · 28 April 2026 · 45:23–53:00. **Evidence:** Wealthsimple hiring manager's stated preferences and interview experience.

Polly values structured storytelling that can move into specific decisions, explorations, and interaction details. She describes spontaneous examination of working files as a useful signal. She also asks for the evolution after shipping; a production walkthrough is a suggestion she says candidates rarely provide.

**Review action:** when interview preparation is requested, connect the story to inspectable details, tradeoffs, and subsequent learning.

**Boundary:** a proposed production demo is not an observed successful candidate example. Do not require public Figma access, confidential files, or an interview deck on the website.

## DC105 — Mig Reyes: show execution before prolonged setup

[Episode 105](https://rss.com/podcasts/diveclub/2011369) · 2 May 2025 · 49:31–56:50. **Evidence:** Duolingo hiring manager's account of their screening process.

Mig describes assessing visual and interaction execution before deeper problem and business questions. His team's initial conversation uses a small set of final-work slides to reduce preparation and lengthy setup. He still values business context and coachability, including for early-career candidates.

**Review action:** make the relevant output easy to reach and inspect; remove setup that delays understanding without adding evidence.

**Boundary:** Duolingo's interview format is company-specific. Do not impose its slide count, no-text format, or visual-design emphasis on unrelated roles or public portfolios.

## DC121 — Dan Winer: distinguish results from a measurement plan

[Episode 121](https://rss.com/podcasts/diveclub/2178589) · 22 August 2025 · 13:30–17:15, 42:45–44:20. **Evidence:** Kit hiring manager's preferences.

For candidates without outcome data, Dan asks what they wanted to affect and how they would have measured it. He also values qualitative customer evidence alongside numbers when both exist.

**Review action:** make the intended outcome, available evidence, and unknowns explicit. A measurement proposal can demonstrate judgment when a measured result is unavailable.

**Boundary:** projected impact is not an achieved result. Never supply invented numbers or imply that the candidate controlled access to analytics.

## DC55 — Amy Lima: improve the account through actual feedback

[Episode 55](https://rss.com/podcasts/diveclub/1509829) · 4 June 2024 · 10:30–15:50, 37:55–39:20. **Evidence:** candidate's retrospective account; the episode includes her subsequent start at Duolingo.

Amy describes noting interviewer questions and adjusting her presentation when clarification was repeatedly needed. She reports that the questions became more substantive. She also regrets not retaining some unshipped work that could have demonstrated relevant design thinking.

**Review action:** turn repeated comprehension questions into specific presentation fixes. Consider relevant unshipped evidence when it is available and permitted.

**Boundary:** this is self-reported improvement, not a controlled comparison. Keep unshipped status explicit, and do not imply that missing or confidential artifacts can be recovered or published.

## DC26 — Marco Cornacchia: put the work within reach

[Episode 26](https://rss.com/podcasts/diveclub/1245670) · 30 November 2023 · 13:20–17:10. **Evidence:** candidate account and the host's account of subsequent contact from Diagram.

With a limited body of startup work, Marco made projects directly inspectable and interactive on the homepage. Visitors could understand the work without first navigating lengthy project pages. He reports an influx of interview approaches after releasing the site.

**Review action:** consider exposing a representative artifact earlier when existing work is unnecessarily buried.

**Boundary:** this supports ease of inspection, not a required homepage layout or animation style. Retrospective interview interest is not evidence that every approach became an offer.

## DC36 — Ryo Lu: let the reviewer examine actual output

[Episode 36](https://rss.com/podcasts/diveclub/1326729) · 1 February 2024 · 30:55–31:45. **Evidence:** Notion hiring manager's stated preference at the time.

Ryo asks for work he can see and, ideally, try, and says he notices typography and presentation details. He contrasts this with lengthy accounts of a standard process.

**Review action:** make artifacts inspectable and preserve thoughtful presentation instead of adding ritual process documentation.

**Boundary:** an available demonstration is useful evidence; a live public deployment is not required for confidential or exploratory work.

## DC2 — Steph Engle: give the presentation a clear argument

[Episode 2](https://rss.com/podcasts/diveclub/936885) · 4 May 2023 · 28:20–36:15. **Evidence:** experienced candidate and interviewer's account and advice.

Steph describes making the presentation support a clear account of the designer's strengths. Projects should substantiate that argument. She also describes presenting work that went wrong, using the decisions and lessons to make the story useful.

**Review action:** connect the introduction to specific evidence and meaningful learning, rather than just listing employers or stages.

**Boundary:** this is presentation guidance, not a mandatory website structure. A failed or unshipped project needs honest framing; reflection does not turn it into a successful launch.

## DC13 — Grace Ling: show relevant ability before extensive employment

[Episode 13](https://rss.com/podcasts/diveclub/1077708) · 17 August 2023 · 14:45–17:15, 19:45–21:10. **Evidence:** candidate's account of entering design and joining EA.

Grace describes an early portfolio containing a Discord redesign informed by conversations with users and a game that drew on her existing background. She connects these with subsequent practical experience and her transition into design.

**Review action:** evaluate the actual problem, decisions, and transferable skills in early-career work instead of requiring famous employers or senior-scale ownership.

**Boundary:** self-initiated redesigns need explicit status. This account combines portfolio work, experience, and relationships; it does not isolate a portfolio-only cause of hiring.

## DC93 — Chris Abad: reveal the important decision already in the work

[Episode 93](https://rss.com/podcasts/diveclub/1885275) · 7 February 2025 · 39:15–42:30. **Evidence:** coach's account of an unnamed candidate and that candidate's reported response from employers.

Chris describes a designer whose presentation initially conveyed a narrow UI task. Reworking it surfaced research that changed the product strategy. He reports more callbacks and a different discussion of seniority afterward, using the same underlying project.

**Review action:** surface a verified consequential decision when it is buried beneath screen-by-screen description.

**Boundary:** clarify the real contribution; do not invent a strategic role. This is a reported change in reception, not proof of an offer or a controlled improvement.

## DC46 — Glenn Hitchcock and John Pham: examine the work behind the shell

[Episode 46](https://rss.com/podcasts/diveclub/1432397) · 11 April 2024 · 37:45–40:10. **Evidence:** Vercel design engineers' discussion of hiring preferences.

The discussion values distinctive work that demonstrates creativity, while explicitly accepting a minimal text-led portfolio when the linked projects provide the evidence. The portfolio shell is not the complete assessment.

**Review action:** follow through to relevant artifacts before judging a restrained homepage. Look for what the candidate actually designed and built.

**Boundary:** do not require the candidate to resemble an existing Vercel employee or add elaborate effects to the site itself. A preference for inventive work in this team is not a rule for every engineering role.

## DC38 — Soleio: make learning and delivery concrete

[Episode 38](https://rss.com/podcasts/diveclub/1345819) · 14 February 2024 · 34:10–35:50, 42:20–43:40. **Evidence:** hiring leader's retrospective account and preferences.

Soleio describes a candidate returning to hiring consideration after releasing an application that attracted his attention. He also asks how candidates acquired skills and applied them, rather than only noting their current proficiency.

**Review action:** connect a claim about learning or initiative to something actually made, with honest context about development and delivery.

**Boundary:** attention is not an offer. Do not infer learning speed from enthusiasm, demand unpaid projects, or treat an unshipped experiment as a released product.

## DC148 — Hannah Hearth: credit the team and explain the leadership

[Episode 148](https://rss.com/podcasts/diveclub/2536922) · 9 February 2026 · 01:05–03:20. **Evidence:** candidate's account of her leadership interview process at Vercel.

Hannah describes preparing to explain her contribution while showing work made by her team. She distinguishes recognising the team's work from identifying how her involvement affected it.

**Review action:** for leadership roles, make direction, decisions, and influence traceable without presenting every team artifact as the leader's individual output.

**Boundary:** a polished team portfolio does not establish personal execution, and a hands-on exercise is not a universal leadership hiring requirement. Her wider interview process cannot establish the portfolio's isolated effect on the outcome.

## DC161 — Brandon Jacoby: connect craft to consequences

[Episode 161](https://rss.com/podcasts/diveclub/2749240) · 21 April 2026 · 36:03–38:30. **Evidence:** experienced designer's critique and product-work philosophy.

Brandon describes portfolios that attract designers' attention while questioning whether that attention communicates useful product judgment. He argues that thoughtful craft can serve consequential user and business needs together.

**Review action:** connect an impressive artifact to the problem, constraints, and decisions it serves, especially for product roles.

**Boundary:** this is a perspective, not a measured hiring outcome. It does not justify dismissing visual craft, requiring revenue metrics, or assuming that a polished portfolio lacks substance.

## DC140 — Henry Modisett: look for demonstrated learning and team fit

[Episode 140](https://rss.com/podcasts/diveclub/2449976) · 9 January 2026 · 12:18–14:17, 14:23–16:14, 16:21–17:48. **Evidence:** Perplexity hiring leader's preferences and account of discovering a prospective intern.

Henry describes building a team with complementary backgrounds and skills. For junior candidates, he values examples of invention and self-directed learning. He recalls finding a student's portfolio and reaching out, and says strength in one area can be exciting; breadth is an additional signal.

**Review action:** recognise concrete evidence of learning, including small projects, and relate the demonstrated strength to the role.

**Boundary:** this does not require unpaid work, an extensive side-project archive, or strength in every discipline. Outreach is not proof of an offer or portfolio causation.

## DC50 — Helen Tran: show depth appropriate to the problem

[Episode 50](https://rss.com/podcasts/diveclub/1461662) · 1 May 2024 · 41:53–45:10. **Evidence:** AngelList hiring manager's description of a specific team and candidate preferences at the time.

Helen describes a senior team working with complex financial problems. She looks for independence, curiosity, and sustained investigation, and wants self-initiated portfolio projects to reveal that depth rather than just a familiar app concept.

**Review action:** for complex product roles, surface how the candidate investigated the actual problem and made decisions under its constraints.

**Boundary:** a domain or project title cannot establish depth. Do not require a finance project, universal generalism, solitary ownership, or a long process essay for unrelated roles.

## DC5 — Mia Blume: explain why the work mattered

[Episode 5](https://rss.com/podcasts/diveclub/947244) · 11 May 2023 · 22:28–24:25. **Evidence:** hiring leader's stated screening preferences, drawing on Pinterest experience.

Mia looks for the problem's importance to users and the business, the challenges encountered, and the reasoning that led to the result. A project that failed can still communicate useful learning. Her emphasis gives a counterweight to the craft-first screening preferences elsewhere in this collection.

**Review action:** make the problem and meaningful decisions understandable alongside the output; preserve honest learning from unsuccessful work.

**Boundary:** this is a contextual preference, not evidence that every role needs a lengthy case study. A clear, concise account can answer these questions.

## DC54 — Julius Tarng: hybrid contribution can be collaborative

[Episode 54](https://rss.com/podcasts/diveclub/1500297) · 30 May 2024 · 35:03–36:26, 36:44–38:40. **Evidence:** practitioner's account of moving into an engineering role and collaborating at Linear.

Julius describes using both design judgment and production engineering skills to accelerate iteration. He credits a design lead and describes their exchange around customer edge cases and implementation, rather than claiming every part of the project himself.

**Review action:** make the candidate's actual design and implementation decisions traceable, including how collaboration improved the work.

**Boundary:** this is a role and collaboration account, not evidence of which portfolio caused his hire. Design Engineering does not require one person to own the whole lifecycle alone.

## DC124 — Catt Small: make staff-level influence specific

[Episode 124](https://rss.com/podcasts/diveclub/2212165) · 12 September 2025 · 22:04–25:21. **Evidence:** practitioner's account of vision work at Asana; career context rather than a hiring outcome.

Catt describes aligning with product, research, and marketing on customer segments and business opportunity, then gathering ideas across design teams and refining an outline with her product partner. The contribution includes framing and alignment before detailed interface execution.

**Review action:** for staff roles, recognise a specific contribution to direction, decisions, and shared understanding; retain collaborators' credit.

**Boundary:** this is not proof of a promotion or hiring result. Do not demand formal management or company-wide strategy ownership from an individual contributor.

## DC130 — Ryan Scott: explain value with the evidence available

[Episode 130](https://rss.com/podcasts/diveclub/2315164) · 7 November 2025 · 08:49–13:43. **Evidence:** experienced designer and educator's interview advice and retrospective project account.

Ryan distinguishes a project label from an explanation of why the work mattered. When metrics are unavailable, he discusses other forms of value, including team efficiency and competitive positioning, and uses DoorDash food photography as a concrete example with multiple business reasons.

**Review action:** state the supported purpose, contribution, and observed outcome in language the intended reviewer can understand; consider qualitative evidence beyond a single metric.

**Boundary:** some suggestions in the conversation infer value from continued shipping. A launch or continued availability alone does not establish business benefit. Preserve the distinction between intent, plausible value, and observed results; never inflate certainty to strengthen a story.

## DC112 — Dustin Senos: distinguish a real-use example from a hypothetical

[Episode 112](https://rss.com/podcasts/diveclub/2081864) · 20 June 2025 · 48:54–50:16. **Evidence:** hiring practitioner's preference and hypothetical example.

Dustin says he would value a portfolio showing software that helps people with an actual task. His example imagines learning about a local food bank's problems and building something useful. He still welcomes interaction experiments, while questioning their sufficiency as the whole signal for broader product work.

**Review action:** connect the artifact to an actual user's problem when the role calls for that evidence; distinguish a real-use project from an experiment.

**Boundary:** the food-bank story is hypothetical, not an observed successful hire. The later [vibe-coding playbook](https://rss.com/podcasts/diveclub/2088077) repeats this excerpt; it is the same example. Do not require unpaid client work or assume that a small interaction study is irrelevant to a specialist role.

## DC179 — John Bai: show judgment through iteration and delivery

[Episode 179](https://rss.com/podcasts/diveclub/3095326) · 25 August 2026 · 39:36–40:31. **Evidence:** design collaborator and interviewer discussing desired hiring signals.

Asked what he would prioritise in a collaborator, John emphasises fundamentals, shipping, and iteration toward something people want to use. He distinguishes that judgment from merely accumulating tools, ideas, or speed.

**Review action:** connect tool claims and execution to specific decisions, iterations, and honest delivery evidence.

**Boundary:** this is a stated preference, not a demonstrated portfolio-to-offer result. A shipped product or social attention alone does not establish product-market fit, and a prototype can still support a narrower role-specific claim.
